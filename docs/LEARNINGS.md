# SO-101 pick & place: learnings and mistakes (read this before changing anything)

These lessons are the reason for the safety rules in `AGENTS.md` and for the checks in the app. Paths such as `~/.so101_pickplace.json` are the legacy state files of the classic dashboard.

Written after the first working run (keychain from the table into a 3-inch bowl). Every item here cost real time. Newest, most expensive first.

## 1. Kinematics and calibration

**Angle zero = middle of the calibrated range, not the "centred pose".**
LeRobot degrees are `(raw - (range_min + range_max) / 2) * 360 / 4095`. I once "fixed" this to use raw 2047.5 and the gripper then pointed backwards at the base. Always sanity-check forward kinematics on a *live pose*: with the gripper held down on the table the tool direction must be about (0, 0, -1) (we measured 7° tilt) and the tip near table height. If it isn't, the zero is wrong.

**Table height is a constant: measure it by touching, once.**
The arm is bolted to the table, so the table plane never moves relative to the arm. A hand-taught estimate was 40 mm wrong (-8 mm). Lowering the tip 3 mm at a time until the motors push back gave -47 mm in the arm's base frame (`z_table` in `~/.so101_pickplace.json`). Every grasp also *touches down* (1.5 mm steps, load + position-error check) and reports the difference. Re-measure only if the arm is remounted.

**Camera -> table mapping: do not trust hand-placed points.**
- A 3-point affine always fits exactly, so "fit error 0.0 mm" means nothing. Use leave-one-out to test.
- The gripper hides the keychain, so the visible-red centroid is biased by up to ~10 cm. Never detect the target while the gripper is over it; remember its last un-occluded position or use another camera.
- Judging the gripper tip by eye in a top-down image is unreliable (the body hides the tip).
- What worked: clamp the red keychain in the gripper, let the arm visit ~14 known poses, and fit each camera with `cv2.calibrateCamera` (intrinsic guess, fixed principal point). The fitted focal length is unrealistic (1734 px) because the camera is tilted and the points are noisy, but the table-plane mapping is good to about 1-2 cm, enough for a 6 cm gripper opening.
- With 2 cameras: calibrate both in the same pass, then fuse (weighted average; a fully visible detection counts more than a partly hidden one) and report how far the cameras disagree.

**Wrist roll motor strains against its cable.** Asking it to rotate gave 80-94% torque. Keep the roll fixed (`keep_roll`), give that motor a higher load limit, and align the keychain by hand instead.

**Reach.** With the tool vertical, at ~22 cm radius the arm reaches ~10 cm above the table but not 12 cm. Hover/travel heights lower themselves automatically, but never below the bowl rim (3 in = 76 mm) near the bowl.

**Carry height.** The bowl is 76 mm tall. Carrying the keychain sideways at 60 mm bumped the bowl. Always: lift straight up, travel at >= rim + 2 cm (we use 120 mm), lower, release, go back up.

**Gripper percent:** 0 = closed (raw ~1837), 100 = open (raw ~3298) for this arm. Verify direction with the physical gripper before trusting it.

## 2. Load sensing (use it everywhere)

STS3215 registers: torque enable 40, acceleration 41, goal position 42, speed 46, torque limit 48, lock 55, present position 56, **present load 60** (bit 10 = sign, low 10 bits = 0..1000, i.e. 0.1% of max torque), voltage 62, **temperature 63**, homing offset 31 (sign bit 11), position limits 9/11, operating mode 33. EEPROM writes need `lock = 0` first.

- Poll position, load and temperature for every motor at 10 Hz; expose them in the UI.
- Guard every autonomous move: stop and hold if any motor (not the gripper) reads >= 70% (roll: 85%) for 3 reads in a row, or is hot. "Hold" = command each motor to its present position (stops pushing, keeps holding).
- **Temperature bytes glitch.** One corrupted read said 72 °C (real: 34 °C). Ignore jumps > 10 °C unless repeated 3 times.
- Contact detection (table): position error rises by > 14-18 ticks over baseline or load by > 110-150.
- Grip check: gripper load >= 12% after closing means it is squeezing something; otherwise retry.

## 3. Environment / operations

- **Disk full (ENOSPC) breaks everything**, including macOS's own permission database. Keep > 5 GB free. Only clear *regenerable* caches (uv, pnpm, app updater caches); never the user's files.
- **macOS privacy (TCC) can silently revoke access to a protected folder** (Documents, Desktop, Downloads) for the app you run things from (Terminal, an editor or an AI coding tool). Symptoms: "Operation not permitted" on every file in that folder, even listing it. Fix: give that app access again in System Settings > Privacy & Security (Files and Folders, or Full Disk Access) and restart it. Workaround that always works: keep the working copy of the project outside the protected folders, for example directly in your home folder.
- Restarting the dashboard **does not release motor torque**. Read the real torque state at startup (a "limp" arm that is actually stiff cost an hour).
- `pkill -f "pip install"` over SSH killed the SSH shell itself (the pattern is in its own command line). Kill by port (`fuser -k 8000/tcp`, `lsof -ti`) or pid file.
- zsh does not word-split `$var` (a `for s in "a b"` loop sent broken JSON and nothing moved). Split explicitly: `${s%%:*}`.
- Pi 5 (2 GB): `/tmp` is RAM-backed and tiny (pip failed with "No space left"; use a disk `TMPDIR`), never `pip install lerobot` without a CPU torch index (it pulls multi-GB CUDA wheels), and run the Pi as a lean **camera-only** server (`--camera-only`). Detection, kinematics and arms run on the Mac.
- Two identical USB webcams: use the stable names under `/dev/v4l/by-path/` (they follow the USB socket the camera is plugged into, for example `/dev/v4l/by-path/<socket>-usb-0:1:1.0-video-index0`), never `/dev/videoN`, which swap between boots.
- macOS camera permission must be requested from the **main thread** (OpenCV can't prompt from a worker thread).
- Python 3.12 venv (3.14 is too new for LeRobot). Three.js is vendored in `app/vendor` so the Pi works offline.
- Don't open the user's Mac microphone. Voice features belong on the Pi.

## 4. UX lessons (the user was right every time)

- **Nothing may scroll.** One screen; camera(s) pinned, tabs below, pop-ups for rare settings.
- **Never fail silently.** Camera lost -> overlay + toast + auto-reconnect. A black picture with no message is a bug. A server that can't read its own files looks "dead".
- **Show the live reason for every wait** ("tilt 83°, needs < 50°"), not a full progress bar that never completes.
- Confirm pop-ups with no follow-up feedback are dead ends. Prefer inline state.
- Physical instructions need a **picture and numbered steps**; a button that doesn't exist on screen is worse than no instruction.
- Things on the table get moved (the bowl moved five times). Detect them every run (the bowl is found by colour: pink hue ~166-170 with low saturation vs the table's blue-white hue ~107). Never store object positions by hand.
- "✅ goal reached" came from a stale bowl circle. Verify success from the picture, not from a flag.

## 5. Process lessons

- Test algorithms **offline on real recorded data** before moving hardware (leave-one-out, FK on a live pose, reach table).
- Make the arm stop for unexpected load *before* it can do damage; it caught the roll motor twice.
- One successful run is not reliability. Report honestly when the user helped.
- Commit often. Don't leave patches only on a machine that can lose access to its files.

## 7. The bowl is an obstacle: a model of its real shape, not a blanket circle
The bowl is 3 inches (76 mm) tall and the gripper must **never** touch it. The first rule was a 13 cm cylinder built from the pink *floor* blob (radius 7 cm, wrong centre): it also blocked a keychain that was clearly reachable (user: "your keep-out is horrible").
Now (`pickplace.py`: `_fit_rim`, `bowl_gap`, `bowl_blocked`, `_check_path`, `_noentry`, `_draw_bowl`):
- The **rim circle** is fitted from the bowl's outline in each camera (loose colour limits, RANSAC in table coordinates at rim height, both cameras fused). Measured: rim radius ~84 mm; both cameras agree to ~1 cm. The old blob centre was ~16 mm off.
- The wall is a **cone** (base = 0.6 x rim radius, widest at the rim). The fingers reach 4 cm above the tip, so the wall that matters is the one at the top of the fingers.
- The open gripper's footprint is 3 cm along the arm (jaws open radially) and 2 cm across it, measured towards the bowl, plus a 5 mm margin (`bowl_clear`, adjustable in Tune). Above rim + 2 cm nothing is blocked.
- Every move path is checked before it starts (leaving upward/level from inside is allowed); near the bowl the arm lifts to rim + 5 cm, travels, then descends outside the footprint.
- The camera picture draws the rim (white) and the no-go line (red) so the rule can be checked by eye. Refusals say how many mm are needed and how many cm to move the object.
- The same rule protects **teleoperation** (the follower will not follow the leader into the bowl).

## 8. Never carry on after a failed grasp (the run that "placed" nothing, twice)
What happened: the old check said "holding" when the gripper *load* was >= 12%. But a gripper closed on **nothing** also pushes hard against its own jaws, so a missed grasp looked like a hold. The arm then carried an empty gripper to the bowl, "released", and repeated it. A finger had also landed on top of the keychain (touch-down stopped 11 mm above the table = the keychain's thickness) and the code only printed a warning and gripped anyway.

Rules now enforced in code (`pickplace.py`: `grip_gap`, `GraspFailed`, `_recover`):
- **Holding = finger gap, not load.** Gap = ticks beyond "closed on nothing". Empty ~0-70 ticks (raw ~1840-1900); the clamped keychain ~530 (raw ~2370). Held means gap >= 150 (`GRIP_MIN_GAP`). The empty-closed position is re-measured at the start of every job (close on nothing once) so the check can't drift.
- **Verify at every stage:** right after closing, after lifting, continuously during every move while carrying (4 consecutive reads below 90 ticks = dropped: the arm stops on the spot), and just before opening over the bowl. Failure raises `GraspFailed`; `place()` is never reached with an empty gripper.
- **Touch-down more than 8 mm above the expected table = a finger is on the keychain/obstacle:** lift, don't grip. Each retry shifts the target 12 mm sideways so it doesn't repeat the same miss.
- **Recovery after a failure:** open, straight up, park high over the bowl (cameras see the table again), re-locate, try again, give up after 4 tries with the last real reason. Never "continue" silently.
- **After release,** the cameras must see the keychain in the bowl zone; otherwise it is reported as NOT confirmed (with where it was seen). A keychain no longer visible is reported as "probably inside the bowl, please look", not as success.
- Every job keeps a step log (`/api/state` -> `pp.log`, file `~/.so101_lastrun.log`) so a failure can be diagnosed from facts instead of memory.
- Tested offline with a simulated arm (empty grasp, slip mid-carry, finger on top, success, retry-then-give-up) before touching the hardware.

## 9. The gripper must be PERPENDICULAR (one jaw 1 cm above the table = no grip)
- A thin flat object can only be pinched when both finger tips are at table level. A tilt of ~10° lifts one jaw ~1 cm at a 5 cm opening.
- The IK used to accept a tool up to ~10° off vertical (soft constraint, loose tolerance). Now it is strict (<= 3 mm, <= 3° from the wanted direction) or it reports "out of reach".
- **The model's idea of vertical can be wrong by several degrees** (joint zero = middle of the recorded range). Fix without touching the camera calibration: hold the limp gripper truly straight down with both finger tips flat on the table, press *Tune > Set vertical*. The tilt the model reads there (`pitch_trim_deg`) becomes the target of every IK solution (`Kin.ik(..., tilt=)`), so tip positions, `z_table` and the camera mapping stay valid.
- The arm's plane azimuth is **-pan** measured from the pan axis, which is 38.8 mm in front of the base origin (URDF pan axis is flipped). Using atan2(y, x) from the base origin gave an 8° error in the first version.
- The measured lean at each grasp is written to the run log ("lean +x° / sideways y°") so the effect can be checked from facts.
- `z_table` (-47 mm) is where the tip frame is when the jaws touch the table; the twin draws its floor there. Real jaw end (triangulated from both cameras) sat ~14 mm below the model's tip frame.

## 10. Leader -> follower (teleoperation)
- A dashboard button appears only when **both** arms are connected and calibrated; it never starts on its own. The follower copies the leader through LeRobot's range mapping, at 40 Hz (120 Hz now, see section 23), with a slew limit (gentle catch-up first), load + temperature guard, the table as a floor (approaches within 3 mm, found by search, not by freezing), and the bowl rule. Pick & place and the leader are mutually exclusive.

## 11. Never stuck (the dashboard must always have a way out)
Every "stuck" so far was something that could wedge with no exit: a job that stopped reporting while `running` stayed true (all buttons disabled), a serial transfer holding the bus lock forever, a motor whose protection tripped and silently stopped answering, a server that hung or died, a Pi camera server that nobody restarted. Now:
- **Job watchdog** (`PickPlace._watchdog`): every job reports progress (heartbeat in every move/wait/touch step). No progress for 15 s: it is cancelled and the arm holds; ignoring that for 28 s: the job is cut loose and the controls are freed; one job may run at most 20 min.
- **Always-available Reset** (dashboard, next to Stop/Release arm): cancels everything, holds the arm, clears every flag.
- **Bus lock with timeout** (`TLock`): a stuck serial transfer becomes an error after 3 s instead of a frozen dashboard; the poll loop skips a busy cycle and reconnects after ~6 s.
- **Motor protection recovery** (`Arm._heal`, status register 65): a motor that tripped overload/current/temperature gets its torque re-armed at its present position (max 4 times a minute, then it says what to free); never while the arm is limp.
- **Every way a job can end leaves the arm holding** (`_hold_quiet`), not pushing against the table.
- **Supervisor** (`scripts/supervise.sh`, used on the Mac and on the Pi, also by the Pi's login autostart): restarts the server if it crashes or stops answering `/api/health`. Verified: kill -9 -> back in ~3 s; the motors keep their torque and the server reads the real state on start.
- Teleop has its own heartbeat watch; it ends cleanly if the leader link stalls.
- Run the dashboard with `scripts/supervise.sh`, not `python app/server.py` directly.

## 12. Model alignment (joint zeros) - do it from the real arm, not from the screen
The base mesh bottom is at z = -2.4 mm in the model and the arm is clamped flat on the table, so the table must read about -2 mm. Touch-down read -47 mm at the tip frame (the jaw end is ~14 mm lower than the tip frame), i.e. the joint zeros were wrong by several degrees, which also tilts the "straight down" tool. `Tune > Align the model` fits lift/elbow/wrist zero offsets + the tool length from 3+ hand-placed spots (limp arm, finger tips flat on the table), re-maps both camera models from their old mapping (no new clamped-keychain run needed) and keeps an Undo. The twin's floor is the base bottom, so a wrong model shows as a gripper under the floor.

## 13. The moving jaw can push the whole gripper up (and what to look at before closing)
Seen by the user: when the moving jaw touches the table while closing, it pushes the arm up and the fixed jaw ends up in the air, so nothing is pinched.
- **Detect** (`PickPlace.squeeze`): close slowly in 45 steps at 250 torque, reading the motor positions fresh (not the 10 Hz poll) and the lift/elbow/wrist loads at every step. If the tip moves more than 1.2 mm, the tool tilts more than 1.5 degrees, or a lift motor's load rises more than 12 % while the gripper is still far from closed, the moving jaw is on the table: open, lift, raise the grip height.
- **Prevent**: pinch at `grasp_dz` above the table (starts at 4 mm, the Tune row "Grab height above table"), not at the table. The keychain is 11 mm thick so anything up to ~9 mm still pinches it. Every detected push raises it by 3 mm (max 9 mm) and it is saved, so it learns once. Torque is gentle (250) until the jaws hold something, then 450.
- **Look again before closing** (`see_check`): with the fingers around the keychain, wait for fresh frames from every camera. Seen within 22 mm of the tip = confirmed; seen clearly elsewhere = it was pushed, do not close; hidden by the gripper = close slowly and log "unconfirmed" (the overhead camera cannot see under the gripper). At the start of every attempt `scene_check` re-looks at everything (both cameras fresh and agreeing, bowl rim fitted just now) and warns if the cameras disagree by more than 3 cm.
- Not possible from the cameras alone: re-calibrating the camera-to-arm mapping at grasp time. There is no independent reference then (the gripper hides the keychain). Camera calibration stays the clamped-keychain run (Tune > Auto-calibrate) or the ruler alignment's automatic re-map.

## 14. Buttons must work, say why they don't, and every instruction must be tested first
- **A UI that re-draws itself eats clicks.** The Pick & Place card and the pop-up were rebuilt whenever ANY field of the state changed, including live readouts (tool tilt, finger gap, bowl pixel position that jitters every frame). A click that straddled a re-draw landed on a button that no longer existed: "buttons look active but are not clickable". Now the signature only contains fields that change what is drawn (`stableP`), nothing is re-drawn while a button is pressed, and live values update their own small element. Verified: 0 card re-draws in 10 s idle.
- **No browser pop-ups** (`confirm()`): Chrome can silently block them, which also looks like a dead button. Confirmations are an in-page bar (`askYes`) with Yes/Cancel.
- **Every disabled button is visibly disabled (grey, dashed) and a line under the buttons says why** ("Hover test, Keychain -> bowl, Loop greyed out: a job is running: press Stop first"). The reasons come from the server (`why`, `why_bowl`).
- **Pop-ups cannot scroll** (overflow hidden, "nothing scrolls" rule). Anything the user must reach gets its own small pop-up or button; "scroll down in the dialog" is not an instruction.
- **Test every instruction before giving it**: click each real button with a real mouse in the real page (requests intercepted so the arm cannot move), check the label text exactly as shown, and check the server refuses unsafe calls. Never name a button that is not on screen. Pick targets the arm can really reach (alignment marks 12/17/22 cm; 25 cm was near the limit with the gripper straight down).
- The yellow measuring tape in the camera view is fine as the physical ruler, but its graduations are only ~7 px per cm in these 640x480 cameras: too blurry to read automatically.

## 15. What the hand-held poses taught us (the wrist zero was 12 degrees off)
- Users cannot measure exact centimetres or the arm's centre. Do not ask. Two kinds of hand-held poses carry the information, and the dashboard reads the joints straight from the motors (hands-free: `scripts/record_poses.py` saves a pose when it is held still for 2 s, plays a ding, and photographs both cameras):
  - **Flat** (both finger tips on the table): the model's lean there is the pitch error. Two flat poses 11 cm apart read -13.9 and -11.6 degrees; leaving any pose out of the fit kept the TOTAL at -12.4 +- 0.6 degrees. That is why one jaw sat ~1 cm above the table.
  - **Rocked about one fixed finger tip** (lean the gripper toward / away from the base): lift and elbow zeros come out of this, but with one spot the split between lift/elbow/wrist is NOT identifiable (jumped 5-20 degrees when a pose was dropped). Only the total pitch error is robust.
- A pure wrist-zero correction of -12.4 degrees (nothing else) explains the data: flat poses read -1.5 / +0.8 degrees, and poses 10 and 11 (recorded after, not used in the fit) rock 21 degrees with the finger tip staying at table height (0.0 mm change; it would have been 12.4 mm). The fitted finger-tip point is 14 mm back from the URDF gripper frame (the CAD frame is longer than the real fingers); the same 14 mm was measured independently by triangulating the jaw end from both cameras.
- Known residual: at far reach (~25 cm) the finger tip still reads ~1 cm below the table, so lift/elbow zeros are slightly off. The per-grasp touch-down (load sensing) absorbs it; do not chase it with more tape poses.
- Relative marks along a tape (equal spacing, tape anywhere) are weak: ~11 mm median, 30 mm worst error from hand noise. Rocking about a fixed point is the strong signal. Absolute radii from the shaft centre are accurate but nobody can place them.
- When rolling the gripper to follow a tape that is not radial, flat poses at a different wrist roll only constrain the lean ALONG the jaw line: use the poses at the operating roll for the pitch.
- `Tune` is not the only way: the state `pitch_trim_deg` (IK target lean) and `apply_offsets` (wrist/elbow/lift zero + camera re-map + undo) exist; this alignment used `apply_offsets` with wrist -12.4 degrees, table -20 mm (tip frame) and trim 0.

## 16. A warning that does not stop the arm is a bug (cameras 99 mm apart)

- 2026-10-30 02:16: the log said "cameras agree within 99 mm ... WARNING: the camera mapping may be off" and the arm went anyway, to the average of two disagreeing cameras (a spot beside the keychain), four times in a row. A camera had been moved and not re-calibrated.
- Rule: if two independent sensors disagree by more than their calibration error, **refuse to move** and say why in plain words; never average them. `CAM_AGREE = 30 mm` in `app/pickplace.py`, checked in `_job` right after `locate()`, tested in `tests/test_camera_gate.py`.
- Open: when they disagree the engine cannot tell WHICH camera moved. A cheap detector would project the arm's known fingertip into each camera and compare; not built yet.
- After any camera is touched, re-run camera calibration before the next pick.
- **Moved-camera detection (added the same night).** Every camera's picture is compared with the picture saved when it was calibrated (`~/.so101_camref/camN.png`, written at the end of Auto-calibrate and after a clean re-map): ORB features + RANSAC similarity on the still background, so the moving arm/keychain/bowl are outliers. Shift > 10 px (about 10 mm on the table) = moved. Checked at dashboard start, every 15 s and when a run starts; a moved camera or two cameras more than 30 mm apart greys out Hover test / Keychain -> bowl / Loop with the reason. Tested on the real pictures: unmoved 0.3-0.6 px, moved 14-16 px, 5 px nudge = not moved (`tests/test_cam_moved.py`). Limit: needs a reference picture, so it only works after the next calibration; until then only the two-camera disagreement check can tell.
- **Re-map = remove the old points first, but it does NOT fix a moved camera.** Old table points belong to the old camera position and wreck the fit, so the Pick & Place card has `Remove the N old points (then Re-map starts fresh)` and the Re-map screen has `Undo last point` and `Remove all old points`. BUT the table points only feed a fallback map (`H`): `px2xy` ignores it while a camera has a calibrated model (from Auto-calibrate). The user cleared, taught 6 points (fit 7.1 mm), and picking stayed blocked (cameras ~100 mm apart) because the camera models were untouched. Only Auto-calibrate repairs a moved camera.
- **Tried and rejected: re-fit camera 1 from the 6 table points.** On the real points the new model differed from the old by 65-90 mm (so camera 1 did move), but a hidden-truth simulation (known moved camera, 6 points, 1.5 px click noise, 2 mm arm noise, 20 trials) showed the pose is badly conditioned: median error 5 mm but worst case 83 mm, with a pixel error of only 2-4 px, and a leave-one-out guard refused 18 of 20 fits. Six near-coplanar points cannot pin a 6-DOF camera pose; Auto-calibrate uses 21 poses at different heights. Do not ship that shortcut. Also: never refresh a camera's reference picture unless its MODEL was just calibrated, or a stale model would look "not moved".
- Status messages were clipped to two lines (`max-height:3.2em`), so the reason a run refused was unreadable. Never clip the one line that explains why nothing happens.

## 17. Never ask a person to act near a STIFF robot (make it limp first, lowered, then ask)

- 2026-10-30: I asked the owner to take their hands away, then to place a keychain between the fingers of an arm that was powered and holding. The owner: "the robot is too stiff, never ask me to do anything when the robot is stiff; same in the dashboard and save it everywhere".
- Rule: check the REAL state (`pp.limp`). If stiff, do not ask for touch/hold/place. Lower the arm first and then let it go limp (`PP.rest()`, `POST /api/pp/rest`, button `Release arm`; `tests/test_rest.py`): releasing from height drops the arm, holding it asks the person to take a stiff arm in their hand. Only when limp, give the person's step; before stiffening, tell them to take their hands away.
- Dashboard audit done: removed "Hold the gripper with one hand first" (Release arm, twice), "Take the gripper in one hand and press Release" (Align card step 1), "hold the follower arm with your other hand" (Re-map step 1) and "The red keychain must be clamped in the gripper" before Auto-calibrate. New: `Keychain in the gripper` row in `Tune…` (arm limp -> person lays the keychain, moves the limp arm so the open fingers straddle it -> `Hands are off: close the gripper` -> held-check), `Auto-calibrate` is disabled until a keychain is held (`kc_held`: finger gap 150..900 ticks).
- Saved in: `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, this file, `docs/design/physical-instructions.md` (rule 13) and the `write-physical-instructions` skill.

## 18. First camera calibration after the camera move (2026-10-30 03:08): agrees with itself, still misses the keychain

**What was done** (all hands-off for the robot part): arm lowered and made limp (`PP.rest`), the person put the keychain between the open fingers and took their hands away, `grip 0` held it (finger gap 262), `Auto-calibrate` ran 21 poses (x 150/200/250, y -60/-120/-170 mm, tip 40/70/110 mm above the table), 4 s of motor fault flags (`shoulder_pan` overload/temperature, `elbow_flex` current/overload) were cleared by the healer, motor temperatures stayed 33-37 C.
**Result:** camera 1 11.1 px from 17 poses (it saw the keychain in 17 of 21), camera 2 1.1 px from only 9 poses (it saw it in 9: the arm hides the held keychain from the side camera; 1.1 px from 9 points is probably over-fitted). Reference pictures saved (`~/.so101_camref`), `cam_chk` = ok for both.
**Verified good:** with the keychain lying on the table the cameras now agree within 8 mm (was 95-130 mm), triangulation gap 3 mm, keychain height 5 mm above the table (was -77 mm). That is independent evidence that both models are about right relative to each other and to the table plane.
**Verified bad:** the real fingertips do not land on the keychain. Low hover at the camera-estimated centre (188, -103) mm, 25 mm above the table, zoomed pictures from both cameras: the fingertips are about 26 mm (camera 1) and 33 mm (camera 2) from the keychain's centre, towards the ring end. The cameras' own estimate of where the real fingertips are (171, -83) differs from forward kinematics (188, -103) by about (-17, +20) mm, so the camera models are shifted relative to the arm by about 26 mm at this spot while agreeing with each other. A full `Keychain -> bowl` run then failed honestly 4 times (the fingers landed on the keychain and pushed it 38-42 mm; one attempt closed on it and it slipped out, gap 20). The checks (see_check, slip detection, give-up after 4) behaved correctly; no motor fault, nothing was dropped on the bowl. A hand-set target shift of (+19, -20) mm (`off_x/off_y`) did not clearly help and was reset to 0.
**Likely cause (unconfirmed):** Auto-calibrate pairs the arm's tip position with the PIXEL OF THE VISIBLE PART of the held keychain.
