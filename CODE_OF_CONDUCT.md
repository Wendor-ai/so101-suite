# Code of Conduct

This is a friendly place to learn about robots. Many people here have just opened their first kit, and some have never coded. Everyone who takes part, in issues, pull requests, discussions and any other space for this project, agrees to the following.

## What we ask

- **Be kind and patient.** Every question is a good question. Nobody was born knowing which jumper goes on B.
- **Be respectful.** Disagree with ideas, not people. Assume good intent, and say it plainly when something landed badly.
- **Help people succeed.** Explain in plain words. Link to the docs. Say what you tried.
- **Say what you did not test.** Honest reports of what did not work are useful, not embarrassing.
- **Keep people safe.** A robot arm can pinch and drop. Do not share advice that skips safety steps, and do not pressure anyone to run something they are unsure about.
- **Respect privacy.** Do not post other people's personal information. Remove serial numbers, network addresses and names from logs and photos before you share them.

## What we do not accept

Harassment, insults, personal attacks, discrimination, sexual or threatening language or images, trolling, sharing private information without consent, and anything else that would make a newcomer feel unwelcome or unsafe.

## What happens if it is broken

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, close or lock threads, and block people who repeatedly or seriously break these rules. We will act fairly, say what was wrong, and prefer a conversation to a ban where we can.

## Reporting

If you are treated badly or see something wrong, tell a maintainer privately: use GitHub's private reporting for this repository (Security tab, Report a vulnerability) or message a maintainer through their GitHub profile. We will keep your report confidential and will not tolerate retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith.

This code applies to this project's spaces and to anyone representing it in public. Thank you for being someone others are glad to meet.
